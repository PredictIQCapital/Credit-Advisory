Credit Readiness Advisory

Creditworthy SMEs get rejected
for fixable reasons.

Product and technology overview — for a software developer and an investor

A credit-readiness advisory for German and Austrian SMEs (10–249 employees). It diagnoses exactly which factors pull a company's file below a lender's approval line, separates presentation problems from genuine credit risk, and routes the corrected file to the lender type most likely to approve it. A free AI-assisted check brings companies in; paid analysis and advisory follow.

01The problem

German SME lending is tightening structurally, and the companies caught by it have nowhere to turn between their bookkeeper and a consultancy priced for corporates.

40.5% report restrictive banks

Share of Mittelstand firms in credit negotiations reporting restrictive bank behaviour — a record since the survey began in 2017, up 6.5 points in a single quarter. KfW Research, 7 July 2026.

Germany-specific worsening

SME rejection rates rose further in Germany through Q3–Q4 2025, faster for SMEs than for large firms, while other large euro-area countries were flat or falling. ECB Bank Lending Survey.

Nobody serves the middle

Bookkeepers do not read rating models. Premium consultancies are priced for €50M+ companies. Automated fintech lenders do not advise. The €3–15M SME is unserved.

What actually happens to the company

  • The SME learns it has a problem only after the bank has said no — and the rejection reason it is given is usually a sentence, not a diagnosis.
  • A logged rejection makes the next application harder. Reapplying without changing anything is the most common and most expensive mistake.
  • Much of what fails is not the business: a shareholder loan without a subordination agreement reads as debt; an overdraft used as permanent financing reads as distress; a management report six months old reads as weak reporting. Each is fixable in weeks.
  • Nobody currently tells the owner which of the two situations they are in.

02The solution

Tell the company, before it applies, exactly which factors fail and whether they are fixable — then either fix them or say honestly that they cannot be fixed.

Three products, one funnel

TierPriceWhat the company getsRole in the business
Credit checkFree Uploads its annual accounts. The figures are read automatically, the company confirms each one and answers ~12 questions. Instant readiness band (A–E), its three biggest weaknesses, and a plain-language explanation. Acquisition and qualification. Self-serve, near-zero marginal cost.
Full report€390 Full questionnaire (41 questions) plus the tax advisor's 20, the document set, the complete diagnostic, the before/after simulation and the lender-type routing — reviewed and released by a human advisor. Mostly automated, low marginal cost. The volume product.
Advisory€1,500–3,000 Calls, coordination with the company's tax advisor, sign-off on reclassifications, support up to the bank meeting. Flat fee per engagement, earned regardless of outcome.
Success fee optional1.5–2% Only where placement is actively supported. Likely requires a §34c GewO licence — a decision deliberately not yet taken.

All fee figures are planning assumptions. The free check widens the addressable funnel from the ~16,000 companies that hit obstacles each year to the ~113,000 that apply (§9).

Two functions, deliberately kept separate

1 · Diagnostic engine

A transparent rules-and-ratios engine — DSCR, equity ratio, leverage, margins, payment behaviour — flags which factors pull the file below a typical approval line and simulates the effect of each fix. Sold standalone at a flat fee.

2 · Remediation and routing

An advisor turns each flag into an action — reclassify a shareholder loan, fix reporting cadence, swap an overdraft for a term loan or factoring, use a KfW liability-release programme — and matches the corrected profile to a lender type.

The separation keeps the core business advisory — not brokerage, not a rating agency. The SME pays, never the lender, so there is no conflict between "best terms for the client" and "whoever pays the most referral fee".

The distinction the whole business rests on: we say no. Every weakness is classified as presentation, documentation, product fit, collateral — or genuine credit risk. Genuine risk is never simulated away; rule R08 refuses, and one substantive finding outranks any number of cosmetic ones. Those clients are advised not to apply. Polishing a distressed file produces a better-looking rejection, burns the lender relationships and poisons the outcome dataset that is meant to become the moat. Declining the engagement is the product working correctly, and the demo includes such a case deliberately.

03How it works, end to end

The free check — four steps, a few minutes

1 · UploadAnnual accounts as PDF or scan. Nothing else needed.
2 · Read33 balance-sheet and P&L fields extracted automatically.
3 · ConfirmEvery figure shown for the company to check and correct. Consistency checks must pass.
4 · Result~12 questions, then band A–E, top three weaknesses, plain-language explanation.

The paid engagement — the full pipeline

StageWhat happensBuilt from
IntakeTwo questionnaires — 41 questions for the company, 20 for its tax advisor; every question states why it is asked. A catalogue of 14 document types says who supplies each, whether it is required or conditional, and in which format.intake/questionnaire.py, intake/documents.py
ImportAutomatic reading of the two structured sources: the DATEV trial balance (current and prior year) and bank-statement CSVs, which yield days at the overdraft limit and returned direct debits.ingest/datev.py, ingest/bank_csv.py

ValidationRefuses to analyse a file whose balance sheet does not balance. A confident diagnostic from a broken input is this business's worst failure mode.validation.py
RatiosA 26-value ratio set computed the way a German bank's rating engine does it, annualised correctly when working from a mid-year management report.ratios.py
ScoreTransparent weighted scorecard: 10 factors, piecewise-linear over published thresholds, renormalised when a factor is unavailable. Every factor exposes its value, weight and points lost. Bands A–E.scorecard.py
Diagnose10 fixability rules (R01–R10) classify each weakness and attach the concrete measure, its cost and its lead time.remediation.py
SimulateApplies the measures to a copy of the case and recomputes: before/after score and band.remediation.py
RouteRanks 7 lender types — house bank, KfW with liability release, state guarantee bank, factoring, leasing, digital lender, mezzanine/MBG — for both the current and the corrected profile.routing.py

The outcome log is the long-term asset. Early on it is a spreadsheet, not a model. It is what eventually moves the scorecard thresholds from published convention to something calibrated on this business's own placement history — a dataset no competitor can buy.

Three parties, three views

RoleSees
Company (SME)A guided seven-step flow: its own case, its questionnaire, its uploads, and the report once released.
Tax advisorOnly the cases of clients who invited them. Confirmations, uploads, the sign-off sheet — not the company's questionnaire answers.
Advisor (us)The whole pipeline: every case, run the diagnostic, release reports, generate letters, record outcomes.

Permissions are enforced server-side, not hidden in the interface, and are covered by tests that exercise them over real HTTP with cookies.

04Where the AI sits — and where it deliberately does not

"AI reads and explains. Transparent rules decide." That sentence is the product's positioning, its legal posture and its architecture, and it is enforced in code.

TaskWho does itWhy
Read balance sheet and P&L from a PDF or scanAI (Claude) or the local rules readerRemoves the real friction: typing figures. Works on any layout.
Decide which figures are usedThe company, confirming each figureA proposal is not an input until a person has confirmed it.
Check the figuresDeterministic checksAssets = liabilities; P&L result = balance-sheet result. Catches misreads before they reach a ratio.
Band, findings, simulation, routingRules engine onlyExplainable, auditable, legally defensible. Never a model.
Explain the result, answer questionsAI, grounded only in the engine's outputAn owner understands "your equity ratio reads as 10.1% because…", not a ratio table.
Guardrail on every AI sentenceDeterministic filterRejects approval promises, odds and rating vocabulary; falls back to a fixed template.
Release of the paid reportA human advisorNothing paid-for reaches a client unreviewed.

Why this split, and not "an AI that scores companies"

  • Regulatory. A transparent rules engine keeps the product on the advisory side of the EU Credit Rating Agencies Regulation, and outside the EU AI Act's high-risk "creditworthiness of natural persons" category. An opaque score that ranks creditworthiness is much closer to a rating.
  • Saleability. The buyer is an SME owner and their tax advisor. A model output cannot be argued with by an accountant; a ratio can — and being argued with is how the advice earns its fee.
  • Data reality. At two to three engagements a month there will be tens of outcomes in year one, not thousands. There is no training set. A model fitted to that would be noise wrapped in false authority.

Recorded as ADR-001 in the repository: replacing the engine with a model is a legal decision, not a technical upgrade, and must not happen as an incremental refactor.

Why zero dependencies was a deliberate choice

  • The product handles confidential financial data on a founder's machine before it is ever hosted. A small dependency tree is a small supply-chain surface.
  • Everything is inspectable. There is no framework magic between an HTTP request and the rule that produced a number — which matters when a regulator or a tax advisor asks how a figure was derived.
  • It is not dogma: the three places where a real deployment needs real infrastructure are named in the code (CaseStore, auth.py, webapp/server.py), and are additions rather than rewrites.

7.3 Commercial — the risks that actually decide this

  • The central assumption is unvalidated. That one-third to one-half of obstacle cases are fixable rather than genuinely uncreditworthy is judgement, not data. If the true share is small, the addressable market shrinks by an order of magnitude. This is the first thing the pilot measures, and the outcome log is designed to measure it case by case.
  • Conversion from free to €390 is unknown. The free check is an acquisition bet. If the band and three weaknesses are enough for the owner, they may never buy the report. The counter-design: the free result deliberately names the weaknesses without quantifying what fixing them is worth.
  • Acquisition channel unproven. Tax advisors are the natural referral route — they see the rejection first — but they may see an advisory product as competition rather than a service to their clients.
  • Trust. An SME handing its complete financials to an unknown brand is a real barrier, which is why the security page states plainly what is and is not yet in place rather than making claims.
  • Founder capacity. Phase 0 is solo and side-structured. Every paid engagement consumes founder days, which is exactly why the free tier had to be self-serve.
  • Timing. The product is counter-cyclical — demand rises as lending tightens — but if credit conditions loosen materially, urgency falls.

The metrics that decide whether this works

Fixable share

Of cases that hit obstacles, how many are presentation rather than substance? The whole thesis.

Free → paid conversion

What share of completed free checks buy the €390 report?

Band uplift

Measured before/after improvement on real files, not simulated.

Placement rate

Of prepared files taken to a lender, how many are approved?
